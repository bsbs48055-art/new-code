Two ingredients show up on almost every skincare shelf: hyaluronic acid and niacinamide. But they do very different jobs. Here is the simple breakdown of hyaluronic acid vs niacinamide so you know which one your skin actually needs — or whether to use both.

What Is Hyaluronic Acid?

Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a humectant — a moisture magnet that plumps skin, softens fine lines and gives an instant dewy glow. Best for dry, dehydrated or tight-feeling skin.

What Is Niacinamide?

Niacinamide (vitamin B3) is a multitasker: it regulates oil, strengthens the skin barrier, calms redness and evens tone. Best for oily, blemish-prone or uneven skin.

Can You Use Them Together?

Absolutely — they complement each other beautifully. Niacinamide supports the barrier while HA delivers hydration.

How to Layer Them

  1. Cleanse.
  2. Apply niacinamide serum to slightly damp skin.
  3. Follow with hyaluronic acid.
  4. Seal with moisturiser (and SPF in the morning).
Tip: apply HA to damp skin, then seal it — in dry air it can otherwise pull moisture from your skin.

Conclusion

Choose HA for hydration and niacinamide for barrier support and tone. Together they are a gentle, beginner-friendly duo. New to routines? See our beginner skincare guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which goes on first, hyaluronic acid or niacinamide?

Apply niacinamide first, then hyaluronic acid, then moisturiser. Thinner, water-based serums go before thicker ones.

Can I use hyaluronic acid and niacinamide every day?

Yes, both are gentle enough for daily morning and night use for most people.
