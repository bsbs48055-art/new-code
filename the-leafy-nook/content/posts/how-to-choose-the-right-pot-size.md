Picking a pot feels like it should be the easy part of plant care — until you either drown a plant in a pot that's way too large, or watch it stay stunted for a year in one that's too small. Pot sizing has real rules behind it, and once you know them, you'll never have to guess again.

## The core rule: go up 1–2 inches, not more

When repotting, choose a new pot that's only **1 to 2 inches (2.5–5 cm) larger in diameter** than the current one. This is the single most important rule in this article.

Here's why it matters so much: soil holds water in proportion to its volume, not in proportion to how much root is in it. If you move a plant from a 6-inch pot straight into a 12-inch pot, you've surrounded its roots with a huge volume of soil they can't actually reach or use. That extra soil stays wet for far longer after watering, and wet soil around roots that aren't actively drinking from it is exactly how root rot starts.

A plant in a properly sized pot will often look "snug" — and that's correct. Most houseplants actually prefer being slightly root-bound to swimming in an oversized container.

## How to measure your current pot

Measure straight across the top opening of the pot, from inner rim to inner rim — not the base, not the outside edge. A "6-inch pot" refers to this inner diameter. If you're buying a new pot to size up, look for one measuring 7–8 inches for that same plant.

## Drainage holes are non-negotiable

Regardless of size, shape, or material, a pot needs at least one drainage hole. Without one, excess water has nowhere to go, collects at the bottom, and keeps the lowest layer of soil constantly wet — a direct path to root rot, even if you're otherwise watering correctly.

If you've fallen for a beautiful pot with no drainage hole (we all have), two options:

1. **Nursery pot method (recommended):** Keep the plant in its plastic grow pot with drainage holes, and simply set that pot inside the decorative one as a "cachepot." Remove it to water and let it drain in a sink before placing it back.
2. **Drill a hole:** Ceramic and terracotta can be drilled with a masonry or diamond-tipped bit at a slow speed, but this risks cracking the pot, so the nursery-pot method is safer for anything you care about.

## Does pot material actually matter?

Yes, but less than drainage and sizing.

- **Terracotta (unglazed clay):** Porous, wicks moisture out through the walls, so soil dries faster. Great for plants prone to overwatering issues (succulents, snake plants) or for people who tend to water too often.
- **Glazed ceramic:** Non-porous, holds moisture longer, similar to plastic in behavior. Choose for plants that like consistent moisture (ferns, calathea) or if you tend to underwater.
- **Plastic:** Lightweight, retains moisture well, inexpensive, and easy to size up as your plant grows. A perfectly good default choice.
- **Self-watering pots:** Convenient for consistent-moisture lovers and for frequent travelers, but can encourage overwatering in drought-tolerant plants like succulents — avoid these for that group.

## Signs a plant has outgrown its pot

- Roots visibly circling the top of the soil or poking out of the drainage holes.
- The plant needs watering unusually often because there's more root than soil to hold moisture.
- Growth has slowed or stalled despite good light and consistent care.
- The pot tips over easily because the top growth has outpaced the base weight.

## Signs a plant is actually in too big a pot already

- Soil stays damp for a week or more after watering.
- Yellowing lower leaves alongside consistently wet soil.
- A plant that seems "stuck," with soil visibly wet far from where any roots are growing.

If this sounds like your plant, sizing *down* — repotting into a smaller pot with fresh, well-draining soil — is sometimes the right fix, alongside letting it dry out more between waterings going forward.

## A simple sizing guide by plant size

| Current pot diameter | Recommended new pot | Roughly how often (mature houseplants) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 inch | 5–6 inch | Every 12–18 months |
| 6 inch | 7–8 inch | Every 18–24 months |
| 8–10 inch | 10–12 inch | Every 2–3 years |
| 12+ inch (large floor plants) | Top-dress instead of repotting | Refresh top 2 inches of soil yearly |
