About The Leafy Nook

Small spaces, big plant collections

The Leafy Nook started from a simple, familiar frustration: most plant care advice is written for people with big, sunny gardens and rooms full of south-facing windows. If you live in an apartment with one small window, a north-facing bedroom, or a rental where drilling into the walls isn’t an option, a lot of that advice just doesn’t translate.

We started this site to write the guides we wish had existed when we were troubleshooting our own overwatered pothos, wondering why our monstera’s leaves wouldn’t split, and trying to figure out whether “low light tolerant” on a plant tag actually meant anything.

What you’ll find here

  • Plant Care Basics — the fundamentals: watering, soil, light, humidity, fertilizing, repotting, and propagation, explained clearly and without unnecessary jargon.
  • Plant Guides — in-depth, species-specific care guides for the most popular, small-space-friendly houseplants.
  • Small-Space Living — layout, design, and vertical gardening ideas specifically for apartments, rentals, and rooms without much natural light.
  • Problem Solving — troubleshooting guides for the things that actually go wrong: overwatering, pests, dying plants, and the difference between symptoms that look alike but have very different causes.
  • Tools & Gear — honest, practical recommendations for the pots, lights, and tools that make a real difference, without pushing products you don’t need.

Our approach

Every guide on this site is written to be genuinely useful for someone dealing with a real plant problem right now, not just written to rank on a search engine. We favor specific, actionable advice (exact watering frequencies, specific soil ratios, step-by-step processes) over vague generalities, and we’re upfront when something depends on your specific conditions rather than pretending there’s a single universal answer.

We are not horticultural scientists, botanists, or professional growers — we’re enthusiastic, research-driven plant owners who have made plenty of our own mistakes along the way (RIP to more than one calathea) and write from that combination of research and hands-on experience. For anything involving pet safety, plant toxicity, or a plant health issue beyond what a home grower can reasonably troubleshoot, we always recommend consulting a veterinarian, a local extension office, or a professional horticulturist — see our Disclaimer for more detail on how to use the information on this site responsibly.
